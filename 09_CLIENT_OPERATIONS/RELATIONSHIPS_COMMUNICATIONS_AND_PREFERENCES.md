# Relationships, Communications, and Preferences

## Contacts and stakeholders

A contact record represents a person or functional endpoint and must include canonical client scope, legal-entity scope, applicable engagement scope, role, declared authority, authority basis/reference, validity period, status, classification, source, freshness, and audit history.

Roles distinguish sponsor, contracting contact, subject-matter contributor, data owner, reviewer, recipient, billing contact, legal contact, and observer. One role never implies another. Influence, seniority, relationship strength, prior approval, email participation, or CRM ownership cannot establish authority.

Hidden, disputed, departed, replaced, or stale stakeholders are marked and trigger revalidation of affected communication, scope, decision, and delivery assumptions. Unknown recipient authority blocks disclosure or external action.

## Interaction history

Interaction records are append-only operational events: time, participants, channel descriptor, purpose, engagement association, classification, factual summary, commitments claimed, follow-up proposal, creator, source reference, and audit reference. Corrections create linked versions; they do not overwrite history.

An interaction record proves only that an interaction was recorded. It does not prove a client's claim, consent, scope change, approval, professional conclusion, delivery acceptance, or evidentiary truth. Those require their canonical processes and records.

Relationship sentiment, influence, health, or priority labels are declared subjective operational metadata with method, author, and as-of date. They cannot control confidence, QA, risk, challenge, or recommendations.

## Communication records

Communication records contain metadata and, only where separately authorized, a reference to content held in an approved boundary. Required metadata includes sender/recipient identities, verified authority status, client and engagement scope, purpose, classification, channel descriptor, time, content reference, retention rule, response status, and audit reference.

This architecture does not send, receive, monitor, draft for release, or authorize communication. A communication record is not proof of delivery, receipt, acceptance, consent, approval, release, publication, or contract formation unless the separately canonical record establishes that fact.

Unknown recipients, mixed-client recipients, unauthorized distribution, classification mismatch, or foreign engagement content blocks use and invokes incident/escalation handling as applicable.

## Preferences

Preferences may cover scheduling, language, accessibility, format, communication cadence, naming, meeting protocol, and other non-substantive operations. Each preference states scope, owner, source, classification, effective dates, priority, conflicts, and status.

Preferences cannot alter facts, evidence, methods, calculations, confidence, conclusions, dissent, risk, QA, legal duties, professional standards, required disclosures, limitations, or approval gates. A preference to omit or soften Material content is recorded as pressure, rejected, and routed to Layers 01 and 06.

Conflicting or stale preferences fail closed for affected use. The safer applicable control prevails until Human Principal resolution, but no resolution may waive certified truth or assurance requirements.
