# Risk Taxonomy, Classification, and Lifecycle

## Identification and taxonomy

Risk identification records the condition, threatened objective, cause, event, consequence, affected scope, source, uncertainty, detection method, and exact evidence/analysis references. A hypothesis or client assertion remains labeled and cannot become a verified risk fact without Layer 04 support.

Taxonomy is multi-dimensional and versioned: strategic, financial, operational, legal/regulatory, contractual, professional, security/privacy, confidentiality, technology/model, data/evidence, client/engagement, reputational, people, third-party, continuity, jurisdictional, and governance. Unclassified Material risk is blocked; labels do not determine authority.

## Classification and assessment scales

Each assessment keeps severity, likelihood, impact dimensions, time horizon, velocity, detectability, exposure, materiality, uncertainty, methodology, scale/version, assumptions, limitations, and sensitivity separate. Qualitative and quantitative measures disclose units and ranges. Unknown values remain unknown and default to the more restrictive plausible treatment.

Inherent risk is assessed before credited controls. Residual risk is assessed after exact current control references and independently supported effectiveness. Control existence is not effectiveness; planned controls receive no reduction credit.

## Ownership and lifecycle

Risk ownership requires an accountable human role with exact scope; ownership is not risk acceptance or approval authority. Lifecycle states are `IDENTIFIED`, `ASSESSMENT_PENDING`, `ASSESSED`, `TREATMENT_PENDING`, `MONITORED`, `ESCALATED`, `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, `CLOSED`, `REVOKED`, and `SUPERSEDED`. Closure requires exact resolution evidence, current assessments, canonical authority, residual-risk disposition, and audit. Reopening creates a new version and preserves history.

Freshness is trigger- and time-based. Evidence invalidation, control change, incident, scope/client/entity/engagement/jurisdiction/confidentiality change, policy or contract change, new conflict, decision expiry, or material assumption change immediately suspends reliance and requires revalidation.
