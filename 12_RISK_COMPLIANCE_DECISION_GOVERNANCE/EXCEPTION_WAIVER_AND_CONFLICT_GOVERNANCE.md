# Exception, Waiver, and Conflict Governance

## Exceptions

An Exception Request identifies exact control/requirement, prohibition basis, reason, alternatives, risk, affected scope, requested duration, compensating controls, monitoring, owner, dependencies, conflicts, reversibility, and closure plan. A request grants nothing.

An Exception Decision records exact request/version, permitted or rejected disposition, Human Principal authority where required, qualified reviews, conditions, scope, start/expiry, monitoring, revocation triggers, residual risk, and audit. Expired, violated, stale, superseded, revoked, or scope-mismatched exceptions block reliance. Reuse requires a new request; precedent and similarity confer no authority.

Requester and decision authority must resolve to distinct effective actors under the mandatory SOD baseline. Neither a request nor an Exception Decision may satisfy its own approval, enter another decision as an approval, or convert exception state into `APPROVED` decision state.

## Waivers

A Waiver Record may represent only a waiver permitted by law, contract, and certified policy. It is explicit, narrow, attributable, version-specific, time-bound where applicable, monitored, reversible where possible, and non-precedential. Scope expansion requires a new request and approval.

Waiver requester and approval authority must be distinct effective actors. The Waiver Record binds the exact approval requirement and Approval Record; it cannot approve itself or serve as approval authority. Missing identity resolution, approval, SOD validation, or non-waivable validation blocks the waiver.

No exception or waiver can waive client/engagement isolation, confidentiality, provenance, Layer 04 evidence authority, Human Principal authority, auditability, mandatory legal or contractual constraint, mandatory qualified review, required SOD, truth preservation, or prohibition on unauthorized external action.

## Conflicts

Conflict Records cover authority, interest, duty, evidence, analysis, risk, policy, contract, jurisdiction, client/entity, approval, SOD, and decision conflicts. They preserve all positions, sources, affected scopes, materiality proposals, owners, recusals, containment, required reviewers, escalation, and resolution references.

Material or unresolved conflicts block the affected decision. Layer 12 cannot conceal, soften, average, vote away, self-resolve, or select the commercially convenient position. Escalation routes to canonical owners; it is not resolution or approval.
