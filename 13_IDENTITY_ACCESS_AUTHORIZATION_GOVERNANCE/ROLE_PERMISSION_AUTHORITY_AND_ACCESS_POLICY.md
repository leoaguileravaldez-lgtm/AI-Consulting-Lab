# Role, Permission, Authority, and Access Policy

## Roles

Role kinds include canonical, client-specific, engagement-specific, legal-entity-specific, professional, administrative, review, approval, temporary, delegated, and service/agent roles. Definitions and assignments are versioned, purpose- and boundary-scoped, time-bound where applicable, non-self-authorizing, and subordinate to canonical owners.

A role assignment grants no permission or authority unless separately mapped through exact valid Permission and Authority Grants. Role name, title, seniority, organization, email domain, CRM stage, client preference, or administrative status cannot infer authority.

## Permission and authority

Permission answers whether an actor may attempt or access an exact operation. Authority answers whether an actor may make an exact governed determination. Grants remain separate, attributable, scoped, versioned, current, revocable, and auditable. Permission cannot manufacture evidence, analysis, professional, QA, release, reuse, workflow, risk-acceptance, compliance-override, decision, or Human Principal authority.

## Access policy and default deny

Policies declare applicability, exact subject/effective actor classes, object/version sets, operations, purposes, boundaries, confidentiality, required roles/grants, constraints, SOD, time, decision logic, and failure behavior. Policy evaluation is conjunctive for Material requirements; priorities or scores cannot compensate.

Canonical default is `DENY`. Missing policy, identity, role, purpose, jurisdiction, confidentiality, grant, authority, boundary, dependency, or audit state never means allow. Conflicting policies apply the stricter valid requirement pending canonical resolution.
