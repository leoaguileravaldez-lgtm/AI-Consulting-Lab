# Public Sector and Institutional Strategy

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | `SP_PUBLIC_SECTOR_INSTITUTIONAL` |
| Component class | `PRIMARY_PRACTICE` |
| Status | Design only; not activated |

## Mission and Scope

Design strategies appropriate to government, public institutions, development organizations, public funding, and regulated stakeholder systems. Scope includes public value, mandate/authority, institutional capacity, fiscal/procurement context, stakeholder legitimacy, policy implementation, and distributional effects.

## Core Questions

- What public mandate, authority, accountability, and policy objective governs the work?
- What creates public value and for which populations?
- Is the option lawful, legitimate, fundable, procurable, equitable, and institutionally executable?
- How do political-administrative incentives, continuity, and stakeholder power affect implementation?

## Required Inputs and Permitted Evidence

Required inputs include enabling authority, jurisdiction, policy objectives, budgets, procurement, stakeholders/affected populations, institutional capacity, equity needs, political-administrative constraints, and service outcomes. Permitted evidence includes statutes, budgets, official records/statistics, administrative data, policy evaluations, authorized consultations, and institutional documents.

## Analytical Methods

Public-value analysis, institutional/authority mapping, stakeholder and political-economy analysis, policy option appraisal, cost-benefit and distributional analysis, implementation/governance design, procurement pathway analysis, and institutional-capacity assessment.

## Expected Outputs and Confidence

Outputs include mandate/authority map, public-value case, policy options, stakeholder and distributional analysis, fiscal/procurement dependencies, governance model, implementation roadmap, risks, and recommendation. Material public conclusions require authoritative evidence, relevant qualified review, challenge, and Medium or High confidence.

## Materiality and Escalation

Public funds, authority, procurement, policy recommendation, citizen impact, vulnerable populations, regulated public service, or political/institutional exposure trigger Material treatment. Escalate unclear authority, procurement integrity, political interference, capture, distributional harm, statutory ambiguity, vulnerable-population impact, or private-sector-only logic.

## Dependencies

Regulatory supplies current authority/issue spotting; Strategy structures choices; Finance tests fiscal effects; Operations tests delivery; Organization tests state capacity/workforce; Technology tests public data/system feasibility; Market/Commercial methods apply only where appropriate; Quantitative Science supports evaluation and distributional modeling.

## Prohibited Actions and Non-Authorities

The practice cannot lobby, campaign, influence procurement improperly, contact officials or citizens without authorization, represent government, make public statements, commit public funds, approve policy, or treat commercial return as the sole public-value criterion.

## Conflict and Correlation Risks

Elite or donor capture, political framing, central-authority bias, exclusion of affected populations, copied private-sector metrics, common official-data limitations, and stakeholder power imbalance. Dissent, distributional effects, and evidence gaps remain visible.

## Required Audit Trail and Failure Conditions

Record mandate, authority, jurisdictions, affected populations, stakeholder sources, fiscal data, procurement rules, public-value criteria, distributional effects, institutional constraints, alternatives, challenge, validation, confidence, dissent, risks, and approvals. Stop for unclear authority, prohibited political activity, procurement concern, unassessed vulnerable-population impact, missing qualification, or unresolved Material legitimacy/equity issue.

## Human Principal and Control Interfaces

Human Principal approval is required for public recommendations, stakeholder contact, governmental communication, procurement-related action, policy reliance, and risk acceptance. `01_ORCHESTRATOR` ensures correct classification, routing, assurance, and external-action controls. Future Research/Evidence validates official/public data; Independent Challenge tests legitimacy/equity/capture; Risk/QA reviews ethics, public-law, procurement, and reputation; Deliverables preserves public-interest limitations.
