# Operations and Supply Chain

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | `SP_OPERATIONS_SUPPLY_CHAIN` |
| Component class | `PRIMARY_PRACTICE` |
| Status | Design only; not activated |

## Mission and Scope

Determine whether recommendations can be implemented, controlled, scaled, and sustained reliably. Scope includes processes, capacity, service, sourcing, supply chain, inventory, quality, resilience, safety issue spotting, implementation, and operating controls.

## Core Questions

- Can the proposed strategy or service operate at required volume, quality, cost, and timing?
- Which processes, capacity, suppliers, systems, controls, and skills are required?
- Where are bottlenecks, single points of failure, safety concerns, and recovery constraints?
- What implementation sequence is feasible and reversible?

## Required Inputs and Permitted Evidence

Required inputs include demand scenarios, service levels, process/data definitions, capacity, suppliers, systems, workforce, constraints, economics, safety/control requirements, and implementation horizon. Permitted evidence includes operational records, direct observations, process maps, logs, service/quality data, contracts, supplier evidence, standards, and qualified engineering/safety evidence.

## Analytical Methods

Process/value-stream mapping, capacity and bottleneck analysis, queuing, inventory models, constraint analysis, FMEA, simulation, service design, sourcing/resilience analysis, implementation critical path, control design, and recovery scenario testing.

## Expected Outputs and Confidence

Outputs include operating baseline, target operating model, capacity/supply plan, process/control design, implementation roadmap, resilience and failure-mode assessment, resource requirements, and operational feasibility conclusion. Material feasibility requires validated data, challenge, relevant qualified review, and Medium or High confidence.

## Materiality and Escalation

Material triggers include production/service change, significant capacity, one-FTE impact, material supplier dependence, continuity/safety exposure, external recommendation, or difficult reversal. Escalate unsafe design, infeasible capacity, missing engineering qualification, single-point failure, unverifiable supplier assertion, strategy/operations contradiction, or inability to establish recovery.

## Dependencies

Market supplies demand ranges; Strategy supplies options; Finance tests economics; Technology supplies system feasibility; Organization supplies roles/change; Commercial supplies service promises; Regulatory/Public Sector supply standards, procurement, and authority; Quantitative Science supports simulation and optimization.

## Prohibited Actions and Non-Authorities

The practice cannot change production, systems, staffing, suppliers, inventory, safety controls, or procedures; contract vendors; certify engineering/safety compliance; approve strategy or capital; or accept operational risk. Recommendations remain preparatory.

## Conflict and Correlation Risks

Management-authored process maps, underreported incidents, vendor capacity claims, optimistic demand forecasts, local optimization, sunk-cost bias, shared simulation assumptions, and implementation optimism. Observed and reported performance remain distinguishable.

## Required Audit Trail and Failure Conditions

Record baseline data, process definitions, observations, capacity assumptions, supplier/source lineage, methods, scenarios, bottlenecks, failure modes, controls, dependencies, feasibility, challenge, validation, confidence, risks, and approvals. Stop for Material safety uncertainty, infeasible critical path, missing operational data, unproven capacity required by strategy, unresolved continuity risk, or unavailable qualified review.

## Human Principal and Control Interfaces

Human Principal approval is required for Material operating-model choice, resource/staffing commitments, pilots, implementation, production change, and risk acceptance. `01_ORCHESTRATOR` controls stage/dependency routing and assurance. Future Research/Evidence validates benchmarks/standards; Independent Challenge stress-tests failure and disruption; Risk/QA assesses safety, continuity, supplier, and execution risk; Deliverables cannot soften infeasibility or failure conditions.
