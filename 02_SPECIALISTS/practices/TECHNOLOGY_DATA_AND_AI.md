# Technology, Data and AI

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | `SP_TECHNOLOGY_DATA_AI` |
| Component class | `PRIMARY_PRACTICE` |
| Status | Design only; not activated |

## Mission and Scope

Determine technology, data, AI, integration, security/privacy issue, vendor, evaluation, and lifecycle feasibility while beginning with the business problem. Scope includes architecture, data readiness, build/buy/partner analysis, evaluation design, reliability, cost inputs, and responsible-AI control requirements.

## Core Questions

- Is technology or AI necessary and proportionate to the problem?
- Are authorized data, systems, integration, security, privacy, and operational capabilities fit?
- Can the solution be evaluated, monitored, controlled, maintained, and recovered?
- What simpler alternative, vendor risk, lock-in, or failure mode could change the choice?

## Required Inputs and Permitted Evidence

Required inputs include business outcome, users/workflow, systems, data classification/lineage, jurisdictions, performance/evaluation criteria, operating constraints, vendor options, costs, and risk requirements. Permitted evidence includes authorized system documentation, data profiles, logs, tests, evaluations, architecture records, standards, vendor documents, threat evidence, and qualified security/privacy review.

## Analytical Methods

Architecture and integration analysis, data profiling, build/buy/partner comparison, threat modeling, privacy/data-flow mapping, AI suitability and evaluation design, reliability/cost modeling, failure-mode analysis, vendor dependency review, and limited prototypes only under separate authorization.

## Expected Outputs and Confidence

Outputs include technology/data feasibility, target architecture, data-readiness assessment, evaluation plan, vendor comparison, lifecycle/operating requirements, cost inputs, control requirements, roadmap, and limitations. Material claims require current technical evidence, reproducibility where quantitative, independent security/risk review, and Medium or High confidence.

## Materiality and Escalation

Material triggers include Sensitive data, production integration, consequential AI decision support, major spend, cyber/privacy exposure, external vendor, or Material technology dependence. Escalate Restricted data, unknown vendor retention/training, unapproved tools, security-boundary uncertainty, invalid evaluation, unreliable model behavior, regulatory uncertainty, or technical-commercial mismatch.

## Dependencies

The accountable business practice owns value. Commercial/Market establish customer need; Strategy establishes fit; Finance establishes economics; Operations establishes operating feasibility; Organization establishes adoption; Regulatory/Public Sector establish jurisdiction/authority; Quantitative Science supports evaluation and modeling without self-validation.

## Prohibited Actions and Non-Authorities

The practice cannot deploy, access production, transfer data, use credentials, onboard vendors, accept terms, change security controls, contact providers, approve architecture/business strategy, accept cyber/privacy risk, or authorize consequential automated decisions. Technical feasibility does not establish commercial or financial viability.

## Conflict and Correlation Risks

Vendor narrative capture, benchmark leakage, shared model/provider failure, tool enthusiasm, hidden training/retention, technology prestige, same-model validation, and weak end-user evidence. Provider diversity alone does not prove independence.

## Required Audit Trail and Failure Conditions

Record problem/outcome, system/data boundaries, classifications, architecture decisions, vendor/source evidence, model/tool/version, evaluations, datasets, metrics, threats, costs, assumptions, dependencies, challenge, validation, confidence, controls, approvals, and stop conditions. Stop for unauthorized data/tool, unknown sensitive-data handling, invalid evaluation, Material unmitigated security risk, missing qualified review, or inability to reproduce decision-driving results.

## Human Principal and Control Interfaces

Human Principal approval is required for data/tool access, external vendors, Material architecture direction, prototype/pilot, deployment, production change, sensitive transfer, and risk acceptance. `01_ORCHESTRATOR` controls permissions, routing, assurance, and external gateway boundaries. Future Research/Evidence validates current claims/specifications; Independent Challenge tests necessity and alternatives; Risk/QA independently reviews security/privacy/reliability; Deliverables cannot convert a prototype into a production-readiness claim.
