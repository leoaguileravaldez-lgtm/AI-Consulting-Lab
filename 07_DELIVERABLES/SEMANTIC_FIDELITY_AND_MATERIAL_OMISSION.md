# Semantic Fidelity and Material Omission

## Transformation classification

Every change is classified as `FORMAT_ONLY`, `NON_MATERIAL_WORDING`, `AUTHORIZED_COMPRESSION`, `AUTHORIZED_EXPANSION`, `REDACTION_ONLY`, `MATERIAL_SEMANTIC_CHANGE`, or `UNDETERMINED`.

Only a proven non-semantic transformation remains presentation work. Wording, ordering, emphasis, omission, titles, captions, footnotes, disclosure placement, visual prominence, chart framing, redaction, summarization and compression require semantic comparison and are never presentation-only by default. `UNDETERMINED` is treated as potentially Material.

Comparison tests proposition polarity, epistemic strength, claim type, modality, causality, confidence, uncertainty, scope, jurisdiction, time, population, units, assumptions, scenarios, dependencies, recommendation strength, limitations, dissent, risk and professional status.

## Claim-strength control

Rendered language cannot exceed the strongest exact upstream support. `guaranteed`, `certain`, `proven`, `validated`, `certified`, `approved`, `fully compliant`, `risk-free`, `best`, `only`, `market-leading`, `no competition` and `will succeed` require explicit upstream support matching their exact scope. Approval may not be represented as analytical validation; QA satisfaction may not be represented as recommendation correctness.

## Material disclosure veto

Clean artifact readiness is categorically prohibited when omission, compression, redaction, wording, emphasis, visual hierarchy, chart framing, staleness, version conflict, inconsistent numbers, missing dissent/risk/professional limitation, unsupported claim strength, hidden content or unresolved equivalence could materially mislead.

No aesthetic score, deadline, brevity, executive/client/commercial preference or Human Principal preference compensates for the failure.
