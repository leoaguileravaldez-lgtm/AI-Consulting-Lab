# Experience Patterns and Lessons Learned

Experience patterns remain probabilistic, conditional, scoped, challengeable, non-universal and non-statistical unless backed by governed statistical evidence. They may guide inquiry, hypothesis formation, research prioritization, challenge and scenario design. They cannot become facts, benchmarks, probabilities, causal claims or universal recommendations without independent support.

Each pattern records independent observation groups, sample/selection context, geography, sector, scale, period, success and failure cases, alternative explanations, confounders, exceptions, causal status, limitations and prohibited uses.

Lessons separate what happened, why it is believed to have happened, supporting and contradictory evidence, proposed learning, applicable scope and non-applicable scope.

Failure memory explicitly preserves failed recommendations and implementations, invalid assumptions, model errors, missed risks, false positives, stale-source errors, forecast failures, client misunderstandings, operational breakdowns, unintended consequences and later-reversed conclusions.

Promotion review must test for excluded failures and survivorship bias. Failure memory cannot expire merely because it is unfavorable, and success-only institutional memory is prohibited.
