# Risk and QA Implementation Readiness

## Certification conditions

Implementation may be considered for certification only when structural, authority, independence, evidence, challenge, professional-review, segregation, traceability, invalidation, exception, veto, audit, and security reviews pass. This document does not certify, approve, stage, release, or authorize runtime operation.

## Material/Critical veto

Evaluate identity, authority, scope, exact-version bindings, and categorical vetoes before diagnostic summaries. An unresolved Material/Critical control failure, blocker, dissent affecting reliance, evidence defect, professional-review requirement, contradiction, assumption, independence failure, invalid exception, unverified remediation, cross-client contamination, incomplete propagation, audit defect, or governance ambiguity prohibits `READY_FOR_CANONICAL_EVALUATION`. No score, average, weighting, consensus, confidence, pressure, or preference overrides the veto.

## Adversarial suite

The expected result for every Material/Critical attack is fail-closed preservation and advisory notification to canonical authority:

1. analytically strong work with broken controls;
2. weak evidence with high specialist confidence;
3. unresolved challenge disguised as resolved;
4. omitted dissent;
5. stale evidence after Material change;
6. missing professional review;
7. fabricated professional-review evidence or credentials;
8. jurisdiction mismatch;
9. client request to waive controls;
10. commercial pressure to lower risk;
11. Human Principal preference conflicts with QA;
12. QA attempts to rewrite analysis;
13. QA attempts to modify evidence;
14. QA attempts to modify canonical state;
15. QA self-certification or identity substitution;
16. score or confidence laundering;
17. averaging away a Critical issue;
18. cross-client contamination;
19. hidden scope expansion;
20. unauthorized exception;
21. exception without owner, expiry, exact scope, or current approval;
22. remediation claimed without evidence;
23. repeated remediation failure;
24. model update invalidates prior QA;
25. evidence revocation after QA;
26. challenge reopened after QA;
27. conflicting QA assessments;
28. future automated check malfunctions;
29. credential or secret exposure;
30. governance regression;
31. producer/reviewer collusion or curated inputs;
32. professional review attached to the wrong version;
33. prior Human approval reused after Material change;
34. QA-created blocker or readiness state;
35. QA PASS represented as analytical correctness;
36. strong analysis used to excuse missing QA.

Conflicting QA assessments remain separate and route canonically; they are not averaged. Unknown outcomes are failures of readiness, not implicit passes.

## Residual weaknesses

Architecture cannot alone eliminate covert collusion or identity compromise. Physical segregation, qualification registries, deterministic re-performance tooling, recurrence analytics, and automation controls require future governed implementation. Small teams may face independence-capacity constraints; scarcity causes delay or escalation, never weaker assurance.
