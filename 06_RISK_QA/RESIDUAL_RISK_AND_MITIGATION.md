# Residual Risk and Mitigation

## Residual-risk assessment

Residual risk is the canonical risk remaining after verified controls and mitigations, not an average of risk, evidence, confidence, challenge, and QA. Each assessment references exact inherent-risk and control records and records remaining cause, exposure, likelihood, impact, uncertainty, interactions, affected decisions, owner, monitoring, trigger, mitigation status, acceptance authority, and audit lineage.

An unverified control receives no assumed effectiveness credit. `UNKNOWN` effectiveness cannot be treated as zero risk. A Critical component remains Critical under canonical treatment regardless of favorable dimensions elsewhere.

## Accountabilities

- The risk owner maintains accountability for the risk.
- The mitigation owner implements authorized treatment.
- The QA verifier independently tests control/remediation evidence.
- A professional reviewer supplies only the qualified conclusion within its scope.
- The exception approver is the canonical Human Principal authority.
- The risk acceptor is the exact authority identified by certified policy.

QA cannot fill another role merely by recording it, and cannot accept risk.

## Mitigation status

Mitigation status is referenced from the accountable source. QA may record whether evidence shows planned, in progress, implemented, ineffective, or unverified treatment, without changing the source record. Adverse results, failed mitigations, interactions, and unintended consequences remain visible.

Proceeding under a canonically permitted Human Principal decision preserves the unresolved finding, residual risk, limitations, dissent, exact acceptance scope, conditions, expiry, and audit trail. It does not become a QA pass.
