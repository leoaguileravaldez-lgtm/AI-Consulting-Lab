# AI Consulting Lab

An applied AI workspace for turning promising ideas into useful, responsible, and measurable solutions.

The AI Consulting Lab helps organizations identify high-value opportunities, validate them quickly, and move successful experiments toward production. The focus is practical: align AI initiatives with real business needs, build evidence before scaling, and design systems people can trust.

## What We Do

- **AI strategy** — opportunity discovery, use-case prioritization, roadmaps, and operating-model design
- **Rapid prototyping** — proofs of concept for copilots, assistants, search, extraction, classification, and workflow automation
- **Data and knowledge systems** — retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), evaluation datasets, knowledge bases, and data-readiness assessments
- **Production enablement** — architecture reviews, integration planning, observability, cost controls, and deployment guidance
- **Responsible AI** — risk assessment, human oversight, privacy, security, governance, and evaluation
- **Team enablement** — workshops, playbooks, technical coaching, and hands-on experimentation

## How We Work

1. **Discover** — define the problem, users, constraints, and desired outcomes.
2. **Prioritize** — compare opportunities by value, feasibility, risk, and time to evidence.
3. **Prototype** — build the smallest useful experiment with representative data.
4. **Evaluate** — test quality, safety, latency, cost, and user experience against explicit criteria.
5. **Pilot** — integrate the solution into a real workflow with human oversight.
6. **Scale or stop** — invest when the evidence supports it; document lessons when it does not.

## Guiding Principles

- Start with the business problem, not the model.
- Prefer measurable outcomes over impressive demos.
- Keep humans in control of consequential decisions.
- Treat security, privacy, and evaluation as design requirements.
- Use the simplest system that reliably solves the problem.
- Build for learning first and scale only with evidence.

## Suggested Repository Structure

```text
.
├── docs/          # Strategy notes, architecture decisions, and playbooks
├── projects/      # Client or internal experiments
├── prototypes/    # Reusable proofs of concept
├── evaluations/   # Test cases, benchmarks, and evaluation reports
├── templates/     # Discovery, risk, and project templates
└── README.md
```

Create these directories as the lab grows; each project should include its objective, owner, data requirements, evaluation criteria, risks, and current status.

## Starting a Project

Before building, write a short project brief that answers:

- Who is the user, and what job are they trying to complete?
- What measurable outcome should improve?
- Why might AI be appropriate for this problem?
- What data and system access are required?
- What could go wrong, and how will people retain control?
- What evidence would justify a pilot or production investment?

Document assumptions early and define a baseline. A prototype is successful when it produces a clear decision—not merely when it runs.

## Engagement Outputs

Depending on the project, an engagement may produce:

- An AI opportunity map and prioritized roadmap
- A working prototype with documented architecture
- An evaluation suite and findings report
- A risk register with proposed controls
- A production-readiness assessment
- An implementation plan, operating playbook, or team workshop

## Contributing

Keep contributions focused, reproducible, and safe:

1. Create a branch for the experiment or improvement.
2. Add documentation describing the problem and success criteria.
3. Never commit secrets, credentials, personal data, or confidential client material.
4. Include tests or evaluations appropriate to the change.
5. Open a pull request that summarizes results, limitations, risks, and next steps.

## Security and Confidentiality

Use approved systems and appropriately classified data. Remove or anonymize sensitive information whenever possible, apply least-privilege access, and review third-party model and data-retention policies before use. Report suspected exposure through your organization's established security process.
